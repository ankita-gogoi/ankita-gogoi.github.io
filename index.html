10-K Text & Sentiment Analysis

Measured tone, readability and forward-looking language across 100 annual reports.

10-K Text & Sentiment Analysis

Goal

Annual reports (10-Ks) are long, dense documents. I wanted to measure their language at scale: how positive, negative or legally cautious they sound, how hard they are to read, and how much they talk about the future.

Data

100 10-K filings, downloaded and processed in Python (Jupyter notebooks).

Approach

  • Built a word-frequency dictionary across all 100 filings, with every word lowercased (e.g. {'revenue': 5231, 'risk': 4870, ...}).
  • Counted positive, negative and litigious words in each filing using a financial sentiment word list, and calculated each as a rate of total words.
  • Split each document into sentences with NLTK's sentence tokenizer.
  • Flagged future-oriented sentences containing will, shall, may, might, should or going to.
  • Measured readability with the Gunning Fog index and counted complex words (3+ syllables).
  • Computed every measure twice, once for the whole document and once for future-oriented sentences only (e.g. PositivityRate and future_PositivityRate).
  • Stored all results in a pandas DataFrame with one row per filing.

MD&A Extraction

Pulled the Management's Discussion & Analysis section out of every 10-K and re-ran the full analysis on it.

MD&A Extraction

Goal

Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), is where management explains the company's results in its own words. I wanted to compare this section's language with the language of the 10-K as a whole.

Approach

  • Wrote code to automatically locate and extract Item 7 from each of the 100 filings.
  • Stored the MD&A text in its own DataFrame column.
  • Re-ran the full text pipeline on the MD&A (sentiment counts and rates, sentences, future-oriented sentences, Fog index, complex words), so the DataFrame holds results for both the full 10-K and the MD&A.
  • Saved the finished DataFrame as a pickle file so later work loads instantly instead of reprocessing everything.

Earnings Call Scraper

Automatically collected about 200 earnings call transcripts and split them into speaker turns.

Earnings Call Scraper

Goal

Build a dataset of earnings calls that separates what executives say in their prepared remarks from how they answer analysts in the Q&A.

Data

Earnings call transcripts from 10 pages of The Motley Fool's transcript listings, 20 calls per page.

Approach

  • Wrote a web scraper that goes through the listing pages and downloads every transcript automatically.
  • Split each call into its prepared-remarks section and its Q&A section.
  • Identified each speaker and what they said in each turn.
  • Organized everything into two related DataFrames:
    • Calls: EarningsCallID, CompanyName, CallDate, full text (one row per call)
    • Speaker turns: SpeakerTurnID, EarningsCallID, SpeakerName, PreparedOrQA, Text (one row per speaker turn)

Prepared vs. Q&A Classifier

A Naive Bayes model that guesses whether a sentence came from scripted remarks or a live Q&A answer.

Prepared vs. Q&A Classifier

Goal

Do executives talk differently when they're reading a script than when they're answering questions live? I built a model to find out.

Approach

  • Trained a Naive Bayes text classifier on the speaker-turn data from the earnings call project.
  • Used only executives' answers from the Q&A section (not analysts' questions), so the model compares prepared speech with spontaneous answers.
  • Built an interactive webpage: type in any sentence and the model labels it Prepared Remarks or Q&A, along with its probability scores.
